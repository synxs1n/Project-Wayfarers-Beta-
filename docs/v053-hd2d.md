# Beta v0.5.3: HD-2D world renderer

The world maps (towns, fields, dungeons, side caves, forts, the Echo Sanctum and the Archive) are drawn through WebGL1 as a lit
diorama under a tilted perspective camera: the map's own pixel art is the floor, walls, cliffs and town walls stand up as blocks,
buildings become boxes with gabled roofs, props stand up as cards and characters are upright billboards with soft shadows. Lighting,
fog, bloom, tilt shift depth of field, light shafts, colour grading and a vignette go on top. All art is still the game's own code
drawn art; nothing new is painted except the faces and tops of blocks (drawn in code from the theme palettes).

Battles, the UI, the minimap and the full map are unchanged. Gameplay is unchanged: same maps, collision, movement, encounters,
interactions; the renderer only reads game state.

## When it is used
- Graphics Medium or High (`gfxTier()`, including Auto when it settles on them), WebGL available, Settings > HD-2D world On
  (`META.cfg.hd2d`, missing = on).
- Everything else uses the 2D renderer exactly as before: Low, no WebGL (getContext fails), Settings off, a lost WebGL context (it comes
  back by itself on `webglcontextrestored`), and any error thrown by the renderer (it switches itself off for the session and says why in
  `HD.why`). `GFX.off` (tests) also bypasses it.

## Files
- `v3/hd2dgl.js`: WebGL helpers, no game knowledge. Shaders (scene, light map, blur, composite), render targets, a quad batch with one
  28 byte vertex layout, chunked static meshes (`H2Chunks`), a packed sprite atlas (`H2Atlas`), matrices.
- `v3/hd2d.js`: the renderer. Per map bake, per frame camera, lighting, characters, post, the 2D overlay, tap picking, hooks, the
  Settings row.
- `v3/config.js`: `HD2D` (end of file): camera, heights, fog, x-ray, depth of field, bloom, per tier settings, lighting presets per time
  of day and place, weather modifiers.
- `v3/style.css`: three rules at the end (the GL canvas sits under the world canvas).
- `v3build.py`: `hd2dgl.js`, `hd2d.js` before `pathguard.js` (which stays last).
- `v3/shell.html`: one `Object.assign(window.__A,{...})` line for tests (`HD`, `HD2D`, `TR`, `mapPoint`, `worldDraw2`, ...).
- Tests: `tools/chrome/v053a.js` (functional), `tools/chrome/v053perf.js` (frame rate), `tools/chrome/v053shots.js` (screenshots to
  `tools/chrome/shots-v053/`, ignored by git), `tools/chrome/v053dbg.js` (debug views with draw masks, not a test).

## Settings and save fields
- `META.cfg.hd2d` (new, optional): `true` or missing = on, `false` = off. Settings > HD-2D world (On, Off), under Graphics. When WebGL is
  missing the row says so; on Low it says Low keeps the classic view.
- No save key, slot or db schema change. The Daily Trial is untouched (nothing is read or written there).

## How it works
### Hooks (all wrappers, nothing old edited)
- `worldDraw` (outermost wrapper): when HD-2D is wanted it runs `trUpdate`, renders the GL frame and draws the 2D overlay; otherwise
  (or on any error) it calls the old chain. The old chain's own world passes (night tint, dusk grade, Phase 6 bloom and vignette, roamer,
  mimic, legend and stick glow drawing) are replaced inside the GL frame by their equivalents.
- `rebuildBase`: marks the bake dirty (also detected from `WS.base` identity, so callers of the original see it too).
- `mapPoint`: taps through the camera (see below).
- `loop`: marks frames drawn by the game loop (see "Display").
- `settingsBody`: appends the HD-2D world row.

### Display
The GL canvas (`canvas.wgl`) sits in `.wview` under the world canvas `#wcv`, at the same place and CSS size, with an internal size of
`WS.vw x WS.vh` (one pixel per art pixel on Medium and High), upscaled by CSS exactly like the 2D view (`image-rendering: pixelated`).
`#wcv` stays on top, transparent, and keeps taps, weather, ambience particles, festival lanterns, the battle flash and fades (all screen
space, the same code as before, so Phase 5 weather blends show as they did). The GL canvas follows `#wcv` every frame and moves into
the new view when `buildScreen` rebuilds it after a battle; `worldSizeCanvas` is untouched. Frames drawn by the game loop never read
the GL canvas back. A `worldDraw` called from outside the loop (tests drawing frames and reading `#wcv`) also gets the GL frame copied
into `#wcv` under the overlay, so pixel reads see the whole picture.

### Per map bake (once per map, again after `rebuildBase`)
- Ground: `buildMapCanvas` with `drawObjects` stubbed out, so every wrapper still adds its part (ambient occlusion, the fort
  gatehouse...). Two frames for the water animation. The code drawn block faces and tops are packed into a strip under the ground art,
  so the ground and blocks share one texture per frame.
- Heights: `#` dungeon walls and field cliffs, `Z` town walls become blocks; water sinks a little, cloud seas and the void drop further,
  lava glows (unlit). Block tops reuse the 2D wall top art where the 2D view drew a top; faces and remaining tops are drawn in code.
- Props: each map row's `drawObjects` (with every wrapper: dungeon props, boards, boats, gates, entrances) is drawn onto a strip; only
  pixels that were drawn become geometry (per 8 px column extents, merged into quads), standing up as cards on the row's south edge.
  Their soft ground shadows (`shadowAt`) are redirected onto the ground texture.
- Buildings: the 2D building sprites (`m.bsp`) are cut up: roofed houses get a front wall, side walls, gable ends, front and back roof
  slopes and a card for what rises above the ridge (chimneys, steeples); keeps get a box with battlements; tents, the lighthouse and
  anything unusual stand up as one card. The town decor pass (`townDecorActors`: service signs, landmark props, the board label) is drawn
  once and stands as a card on each facade.
- Sun shadow: a heightfield (blocks, buildings, trees, rocks, pillars, tents...) is marched toward three suns (day, dusk, dawn) into a
  soft shadow texture; the time of day blends the three.
- Lights: every 2D light source (building lanterns, wall torches, the dungeon stairs, scanLights' lamps, braziers, waystones, gates,
  crystals, lava, relit runes), placed on the object it belongs to, with its `LIGHTC` colour.
- Static geometry goes into 8x8 tile chunks, one buffer per chunk row; only the chunks under the view are drawn.
- A chest, lever or bars change only re-cut the prop cards and buildings (the ground, blocks and shadows are kept): about 25 ms.

### Per frame
- Camera: perspective, `HD2D.pitch` 40 degrees, follows the hero with a smooth glide, clamped to the map like the 2D camera (a little
  slack past the top edge), focus point snapped to whole pixels so the art stays steady, a slow idle sway (none under reduced motion).
  Distance is set so sprites are exactly as big as in the 2D view at the hero; wide views cap the horizontal field of view.
- Characters: the hero, townsfolk, gates of the Echo Sanctum, bounty elites, lost wayfarers, roaming legends, the Scourge or Echo on its
  circle, dungeon roamers (with sight cone, elite ring and chase mark), mimic glints, gathering spots, shelves, fountains, braziers, wall
  torches, windmill sails, chimney smoke, the quest board mark and the dungeon's way out, all from the existing sprite canvases through a
  packed atlas (uploaded once per canvas), no per frame allocation. Heights keep the art's pixel proportions (`1/cos(pitch)`).
- Visibility: props, walls and buildings in front of the hero thin out in a dithered circle around him, and he shows through anything
  still in front as a soft silhouette. Characters never sink below a minimum light.
- Lighting: ambient and sun (or moon) from presets per phase (day, dusk, night, dawn) blended with Phase 5's `trPhW` and the dusk and dawn
  progress, presets for dungeons, caves, the halls (Archive, Sanctum) and dark realms; weather scales ambient and sun and tints the fog,
  blended with Phase 5's weather fade. Point lights are drawn as soft discs into a small light map each frame (its alpha carries the sun
  shadow of the same patch), so a pixel reads one texture for all lights. Windows, lamps and crystals glow at night. Distance fog.
- Post: the scene is drawn straight onto the canvas with the colour grade folded into one affine map per pixel; the finished frame is
  copied, shrunk and blurred (Medium: every other frame); one blended pass lays the blur over the top and bottom of the view (tilt shift,
  sharp band at the hero, wider on sideways views), adds bloom, light shafts (outdoors, by day, dusk and dawn, not in rain) and the vignette.

### Taps
`mapPoint` returns the tile under the finger through the camera: a tap on a character's sprite picks that character's tile; otherwise
the tap's ray walks the height map (blocks and buildings) and returns the first block or ground tile it meets. Drag directions use the
ground point. Tap to walk, tap to talk and tap a door work as before.

## Reduced motion
`reducedMotion()`: no camera sway, every animated thing drawn at time 0 (flicker, smoke, sails, portals, gathering spot glints, bobbing
marks), no drifting overlay particles, light shafts still; the weather keeps showing (as a still frame, like the 2D view).

## Frame rates (tools/chrome/v053perf.js)
Headless Chromium, WebGL on SwiftShader (software, no GPU), CPU throttled 4x with CDP `Emulation.setCPUThrottlingRate`, 390x844 at 2x.
`fps` is the real requestAnimationFrame rate over 3 s, with the share of frames slower than 20 ms. Two runs, both shown.
Command: `PWHTML=../../out/aurelis3.html node v053perf.js`.

| row | town (night) fps | field (rain) fps | dungeon fps |
|---|---|---|---|
| before: Low (2D) | 60.4 (5%), 60.4 (6%) | 60.5 (1%), 60.6 (4%) | 60.5 (6%), 60.4 (9%) |
| before: Medium, HD-2D off (2D) | 60.3 (4%), 60.5 (5%) | 60.5 (3%), 60.6 (3%) | 60.2 (7%), 56.8 (18%) |
| after: Medium, HD-2D | 50.6 (47%), 47.7 (60%) | 60.2 (6%), 58.5 (12%) | 57.4 (18%), 51.5 (34%) |
| after: High, HD-2D | 44.5 (75%), 41.9 (79%) | 55.9 (20%), 50.1 (44%) | 49.0 (50%), 49.9 (47%) |

Reading the numbers: SwiftShader renders on the CPU, so this is a pessimistic stand in for a phone GPU; the 2D view is cheaper here and
always will be. Medium holds about 50 fps or better in every scene; the town at night (many lights, townsfolk and buildings in view)
is the heaviest. What it took to get there from a first version at 5 to 11 fps: prop cards cut to the pixels actually drawn (overdraw),
point lights into a light map instead of a loop per pixel, rendering at the art pixel grid, culling static geometry by chunks, one pass
over the geometry per frame (the blur source is a copy of the finished frame, not a second render), shared textures (fewer draw groups),
the grade folded into one matrix, the blur remade every other frame on Medium, and never reading the GL canvas back during play (that
alone cost about 20 fps under the throttle). The main thread cost of the HD-2D frame itself is about 2 ms at 4x throttle (the
`ms per worldDraw` column of the script times calls made outside the game loop, which also copy the frame into the 2D canvas, so it is
higher than in play). Bakes: town about 100 to 200 ms, field 100 to 250 ms (behind the fade on entering a map), a chest about 25 ms.
Auto (Phase 6) still steps tiers by the measured frame rate, so a slow device falls back to Low (the 2D view) by itself.

  row (on by default), off and on again; taps through the camera land on the tapped ground tiles (every open tile around the hero in a field), a

## Known limits
- Medium and High render at the same internal resolution (the art pixel grid); High only adds a wider bloom pass and a finer light map.
  A finer grid (`HD2D.tiers.*.px` 2) works but was too slow on SwiftShader to keep, and would need the 2D overlay to match.
- Tall props are cards: from the side they are flat, and a prop wider than its tile can overlap a neighbour's face slightly.
- A building directly south of the hero can hide the street in front of a door; the x-ray circle and silhouette keep the hero visible,
  but the hidden ground is only seen through the circle.
- Tapping the canopy of a tree picks the ground behind it (cards are not in the pick height map); tapping a roof picks the building.
- Dropped from the 2D passes in HD-2D: water glints, swaying grass, critters and birds (Phase 6 ground and sky layers), the festival
  bunting along the road (the sky lanterns stay), the dusk and dawn swallows and fireflies, and per frame shop prop animation (smithy
  embers, alchemy bubbles, guild banners and the inn lamp pulse are drawn once into the facade cards).
- The fort gatehouse in fields stays painted flat on the ground (as in 2D).
- The sun shadow texture is baked per map from fixed sun directions (day, dusk, dawn); it does not track the hour within a phase.
- `preserveDrawingBuffer` is off, so a screenshot of the GL canvas taken outside the frame is blank; the copy into `#wcv` for calls
  outside the game loop covers tests.
